# Which ideas use the national 2008–25 history?

**Lead with how established filers expanded H-2A use. For the AEWR, lead with how much of the program actually posts the reference wage and how recurring jobs change their offers when it changes.** Both questions cover nearly the entire historical program. Neither requires an underpowered estimate of a nationwide hiring elasticity. Two additional pilots test whether local labor markets or agricultural growing conditions predict subsequent certifications.

**What the full-history growth result adds.** Between FY2008 and FY2025 the local series grew by 307,892 positions. Summing consecutive-year changes, 198,836 of that growth came from filers present in both adjacent years. Within those filers, an exact decomposition credits **190,824 positions to more cases with positive certifications and 8,012 to larger average cases**. In FY2020–25 more cases added 107,642 positions while smaller average case size offset 19,195. The case-count contribution was positive in 47 of 50 states over the full period. A separate geographic decomposition puts **179,571 of the 198,836 positions (90.3%) in filer–state pairs already active the previous year**. These are two views of the same growth and their contributions overlap. [National and state calculations](../business/02_broad_growth.R)

Each national case is counted once. The filer is the organization on the application and can serve several farms. For each returning filer, the change in cases is multiplied by its average positions per case across the two years, and the change in average case size is multiplied by its average number of cases. The two terms sum exactly to its change in positions. State results apportion positions across the worksites. They allow a multistate order to appear once in each state served. The total-filer result survives omitting FY2008: from FY2009 to FY2025 the case-count term was +201,769 and the size term −3,928. The FY2008 local total exceeds the published OFLC benchmark by 8,068. [Baseline and linkage audit](../business/README.md)

**What the full-history wage result adds.** Among the 3.34 million covered positions, **95.3% carried a disclosed hourly offer matching the receipt-date reference to the cent**. The case-weighted share was 91.6%. Requiring the same reference on both receipt and decision dates retains 80.7% of all positions and raises the match share to 97.3%. The occupation-matched returning-filer comparison covers 63.0% of all national positions. In those groups a mean $0.720 reference increase accompanied a $0.715 increase in the posted wage. A mean $0.176 decrease accompanied a $0.143 decrease. These are current-position-weighted changes among returning groups. They describe offers conditional on certification and say little by themselves about the number of positions an employer would request at a different AEWR. [Year-round eligibility and calculations](../policy/07_broad_coverage.R)

The wage screen uses a unique, plausible hourly offer, a single supported worksite state, valid dates and a dated reference. It excludes likely range herding and restricts reference years from 2023 onward to the six common field and livestock occupations. The 2009–10 wage-method change lowers coverage to 35.0% of positions in FY2009 and 21.2% in FY2010. This is a comparison to a recorded reference. Other wage obligations, realized pay, and cases that never filed are outside the measure. [AEWR applicability and history](../../../content/contracts/aewr-inputs.md)

## Why the two broad quantity pilots rank lower

**Outside employment.** Freeze each county's construction, manufacturing and leisure/hospitality job shares in 2007. Multiply each share by the prior year's national growth in its industry, excluding the county's own state, and sum across the three industries. County and state-by-year fixed effects compare counties with different pre-existing industry mixes inside the same state and year. Suppressed or missing employment cells stay missing. A predicted one-percentage-point increase relative to baseline private employment accompanied a **0.98-point increase** in actual employment in those industries (two-way state/year cluster interval 0.75 to 1.20). The corresponding next-start-year estimate was **−0.0004 log points in log(1 + certified positions)** (−0.0367 to +0.0360). The 2010 and 2021 outcome years account for 32.3% and 38.4% of residual exposure variance, or only 3.8 equally informative years across the full sample. All three industries can also affect local product demand. These conventional intervals do not account for every source of common industry-shock uncertainty. Related work already links housing-driven labor demand to H-2A use, so a new paper would need to establish an additional contribution. [Pilot](nonfarm.R) · [BLS industry definitions](https://www.bls.gov/cew/classifications/industry/industry-supersectors.htm) · [Castillo and Charlton](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ajae.12320)

**Growing conditions.** Measure summer heat and dryness relative to each county's own 1981–2006 weather, then relate those measures to next year's certified positions, retaining zero counties. Hotter summers predicted lower contemporaneous corn yields. Prior-summer heat was positively associated with next year's log(1 + positions) over 2008–25 (+0.056), but the estimate was −0.012 for 2019–25. Adding county-specific trends reduced the heat estimate to +0.021 (95% interval −0.001 to +0.043). Future dryness also predicted earlier positions. These checks weaken a causal reading even with extensive geographic coverage. [Weather, crop-yield and timing tests](../calendar/04_broad_weather.R)

## Where Washington fits

In FY2025, 434 of 24,526 disclosed cases listed Washington as a principal worksite (1.8%). Those cases represented 34,560 of 398,059 reconciled positions (8.7%). The 71 principal-Washington cases with a positive numeric piece rate accounted for 8,454 positions. Some covered rates appear in attachments, and a piece-rate entry alone does not establish exposure.

Across FY2024–25, all cases naming Washington as a principal site or having a positive allocated Washington worksite together accounted for **0.44% of all FY2008–25 filings and 1.92% of all FY2008–25 positions**. This deliberately counts entire multistate cases and both complete fiscal years, regardless of task or whether the intervention was in force. These are footprint comparisons for the two disclosure cohorts. The number of workers whose pay changed is unknown. Washington piece rates, bond thresholds and occupation-specific events belong in supporting case studies under a broad-coverage priority. [Washington footprint check](washington.R)

## Reproduction

The first two rows use national fiscal-year case records. County regressions use allocated start-year outcomes, which may include later decisions for 2025 starts in the current FY2026 Q4 vintage. A filing equivalent is the county panel's fractionally allocated application count. Positions measure certified capacity and can count the same person across contracts. The coverage denominators above distinguish fiscal-year cases from dated start-year allocations. The pilots were exploratory.

```sh
source scripts/pipeline_helpers.sh
configure_r_library
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/business/02_broad_growth.R /tmp/h2a-broad-growth
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/policy/07_broad_coverage.R /tmp/h2a-history-wage-coverage
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/coverage/nonfarm.R
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/calendar/04_broad_weather.R
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/coverage/washington.R
```
