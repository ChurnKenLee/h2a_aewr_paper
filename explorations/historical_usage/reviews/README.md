# What the two Gemini reviews change for the historical H-2A paper

**The two reviews identified specific filing responses and potential wage interventions.** The surety bond thresholds give a measurable filing-size response among contractors. Matching Washington's crop- and task-specific prevailing wages to job orders could clarify how pay was advertised in a small group of filings. A subsequent coverage audit ranks national filer growth and year-round AEWR wage setting first for the 2008–25 paper. It also tests nonfarm labor demand and summer weather on samples covering over 95% of their respective position denominators. [Coverage-based ranking and new results](../coverage/README.md)

**Use the full period to explain who expanded.** Filers present in consecutive years supplied 52.7% of net position growth across the FY2011–19 transitions and 72.1% across FY2020–25. On the later filings with identifiable worksites, continuing filing organizations still account for 72.1%, while continuing worksite businesses account for 41.2% under the main linkage and 58.7% under a looser linkage. This gives the quantity history a substantive role alongside the policy result. Treat FY2008 as evidence of prior presence and show the endpoint sensitivity because the local FY2008 certified total exceeds the published benchmark by 8,068 positions. [Historical growth and coverage](../business/README.md)

Carbon reviewed possible new sources of variation in requests or hours. Neon reviewed the leading repository results and proposed rival explanations. Each received selected numerical excerpts and a focused question in parallel, with instructions to provide analysis only. The completed sessions used Gemini 4 Carbon at High and Gemini 4 Neon at XHigh through the review wrapper's ACP backend. Their returned texts are available in full. The judgments below incorporate independent repository checks and post hoc tests. [Carbon review](carbon-high.md) · [Neon review](neon-xhigh.md) · [Original historical investigation](../README.md)

## Neon's six proposals

| Proposal | What the check established |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Search for split applications across filers and dates.** | Of 124 late contractor cases one to five below a step, 123 have a complete observed worksite address. Within 30 start days, 25 have a 1–10-position companion that could cross the step and shares state, filing agent and six-digit occupation. The comparison 31–60 days away gives 24. Requiring the same observed street address, city, state, ZIP and occupation leaves **one** focal case, involving a different filer. Its companion exceeds the exact shortfall. Adding a common named agent leaves **zero** among 79 fully observed cases. Twelve focal cases do have an *identically sized* order at the same site with the same occupation and agent, versus zero in the 31–60-day comparison. Those 12 cases, spanning six focal filers, are the best candidates for a paired job-order audit. Address matching used observed addresses. Fuzzy address and phone links across sister entities remain untested. An overlapping address or agent cannot establish a single client order or its counterfactual size. [New companion test](../packaging/02_review_companions.R) · [Original same-day and duplicate checks](../packaging/README.md) |
| **2. Check filing-agent concentration and missing mass.** | The +31.6 percentage-point contractor-minus-comparison change in the share exactly one below a step reproduces on 487 applications. It is +30.0 points among 51 filers retaining their role and appearing near a step in both eras, or **+27.7 points** with equal weight per filer (exploratory 95% interval +7.5 to +48.0). That restriction selects survivors. Only 55 of 91 late contractor hits name an agent. Among applications with named agents the contrast is +22.2 points. Removing the five largest agents by late case volume gives +8.8 points, while ranking the five by early volume gives +21.5. Across the ten local size bins the +31.6 points are offset by −12.4 at the step, −14.2 above and −4.9 farther below. Shares within each group sum to one by construction. This cannot identify missing workers or deliberate avoidance. [Recurring-filer and agent tests](../policy/03_review_bond.R) · [Integer distribution](../policy/README.md) |
| **3. Separate receipt from decision dates and screen other wage floors.** | Among 46,925 applications with both dates within 56 days of the effective date, **1.1%** quoted the new reference when both dates preceded it, **26.0%** when the application crossed it, and **95.7%** when both followed it. Among crossers, 70.8% quoted the old rate. Across 110 recurring filer–state–occupation–update groups observed in all three categories the shares were 0.9%, 24.0% and 98.9%. On the original post-receipt sample, requiring both AEWRs to exceed both adjacent annual agricultural minimums by at least $1 gives **97.5% matching after rises and 76.9% after falls**. The fall sample has 2,895 applications across five update years. A simple decision-date replacement and the observed annual minimums explain little of the asymmetry. Processing times, local prevailing rates, guaranteed offers and amendments remain relevant. Final recorded offers measure reference matching. Compliance and wages paid require more information. [Timing and competing-floor analysis](../policy/04_review_reset.R) |
| **4. Compare duties, refilings and processing delays around the occupation rule.** | Across 1,841 recurring equipment-operator cells retaining the same named agent, mentions of trucking in additional requirements fell **32.6% to 2.6%**. Across 74 cells observed on both sides in the FY2023 workbook the figures are 30.4% and 4.1%. Truncating all records at 500 characters preserves almost all of the decline. Among 700 pre-rule equipment filings that mentioned trucking, **70.9% shared the wording around the first match with at least two other filers**. One agent accounts for 52.3% of the net decline in the same-agent sample. Shared templates are a plausible mechanism. The field records additional requirements. It does not provide a revision history, complete duties or a certifying officer's corrections. Lags alone could not attribute a change to the officer. [Text and templates](../occupations/03_review_text.R) · [Switchers and placebo seasons](../occupations/README.md) |
| **5. Triangulate H-2A with QCEW support-industry payroll and the Farm Labor Survey.** | The ready-made quarterly QCEW file extracts NAICS 111 and 112. The raw county records also contain 1151, including suppressed cells. In a 2024 check, 2,044 US counties or county equivalents had a 1151 row, covering 8,176 county-quarters. Only **1,987 (24.3%)** disclosed every reported ownership component, after excluding state unallocated areas and territories. A decrease in crop payroll alongside an increase in support payroll could reflect a move between employing industries. Neither series identifies domestic workers displaced or hired. FLS publishes regional farm-labor aggregates. Treat these outcomes as corroboration conditional on coverage. [Source check](01_source_support.R) · [Quarterly QCEW producer](../../../code/a01_sources/04_02_qcew_quarterly_employment.py) · [USDA FLS](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Surveys/Guide_to_NASS_Surveys/Farm_Labor/) |
| **6. Try a border-county design.** | The AEWR-region version already has a substantial implementation with leave-pair-out and shared-region inference. The original 25 edges span 16 regions with a leverage-equivalent count of 12.16. Eight mutually region-disjoint pairs survive. The screened 807-county model has a wild-bootstrap interval from −9.73 to +11.09 certifications per 1,000 baseline farm workers per $1/hour. A state-minimum border treatment would require separate assignment and overlap checks. Neon's 15-border and 2% targets are proposals. To compare power one must first define an effect in the same units and allow for dependence across pairs. Meeting a target cannot establish exogeneity. [Border redesign and power report](../../../outputs/reports/border_redesign.md) |

Neon correctly quoted the 4.56% detectable increase, Florida's 42.6% leverage and 3.78 effective regions for the specific **predetermined contract timing × AEWR** test. The earlier-outcome estimate was −1.21% with a 95% interval of −5.44% to +3.21%. The separate contractor total-quantity pilot gave −5.2% with a −4.6% early-period comparison. These checks limit what we can say about a policy-induced change in quantity. The full history still measures program growth, entry and seasonality. ## Carbon's six proposals

| Proposal | Decision after verification |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Exploit crop- and area-specific prevailing wages.** | **Investigate a Washington crop and task first.** The earlier project ledger proposed this as D2 and never ran the search. DOL publishes approved findings, and Washington documented historical posting dates. A state survey identified 81 commodity–activity combinations in 2020, of which 19 met the wage-determination criteria. In the local FY2025 Washington disclosures, 71 of 434 distinct cases had a positive numeric piece rate, 45 of those named apples, cherries or pears in the unit text, and 193 of all 434 pointed to an attachment or additional wage/crop information. Earlier normalized fields have no positive rates in FY2017–19. A positive entry can describe an hourly equivalent. Recover the actual piece unit, task, geography and first effective date from the order and approved finding before testing pass-through. A no-finding can reflect a change in survey participation or work organization, and continuing jobs can retain a guaranteed rate. A national archive with 30 effective areas and a material hourly cost shock has yet to be demonstrated. [Source check](01_source_support.R) · [Earlier D2 decision](../../../markdowns/identification_search_report.md) · [DOL wage library](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/wages/agriculture) · [Washington survey findings](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoDownloadDocument?documentID=169345&eodoc=true&pubId=) |
| **2. Use Secure Communities or 287(g) rollout.** | The cached TRAC facility tables measure annual detainer activity by facility location through FY2021. Neither an activation date nor workers' residence or employment county follows from those counts. Secure Communities began in 2008, the first outcome year, and had national reach in January 2013. Some early adopters have virtually no preperiod here. Historical 287(g) agreements are a distinct treatment with their own timing, coverage and selection. External evidence finds responses around state agreement signing before county activation. Reconstruct both clocks and a pre-enforcement labor exposure before trying a reduced form. Keep enforcement effects separate from an AEWR effect. [TRAC facility definition](https://tracreports.org/phptools/immigration/newdetain/about_data.html) · [Historical agreement data](https://deportationdata.org/docs/ice/codebook-287g.html) · [Research on pre-activation responses](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2510928123) |
| **3. Use the ACA's employer-size and seasonal thresholds.** | **Drop the proposed assignment, Carbon's top choice.** Applicable-large-employer status uses prior-year average monthly full-time employees plus full-time equivalents across related employers. The seasonal-worker exception to size uses 120 days. Six months applies to customary seasonal employment under a look-back method. In 2015 the 50–99 transition relief was conditional. Application headcounts, contract duration and the filer identifier do not recover those conditions. Of 37,490 usable FY2011–16 case intervals, 503 change sides between 180 days and six calendar months, **18.1% of the 170–190-day neighborhood**. Normalized requested headcount is unavailable on all 12,182 positive reconciled FY2013–14 cases. Even perfect precision around observed application sizes would answer a different question. [ACA pilot](../policy/05_review_aca.R) · [IRS workforce definition](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/determining-if-an-employer-is-an-applicable-large-employer) · [Transition rules](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2014-09_IRB) |
| **4. Compare freezes before and after filing.** | The local NOAA files are partitioned by month and contain **daily** county minimum temperatures. The local RMA files checked are annual Summary of Business and actuarial data. They supply no day of a freeze loss. The general 60–75-day job-order submission window and the application deadline of at least 45 days concern different stages. A freeze 65 days before the requested start can fall on either side of an actual receipt. Predefine a county's crop season using earlier years, allow zero filings, use temperature anomalies against county-by-calendar-day normals, and distinguish the effect on filing from changes in the reported start date. A request or withdrawal response to crop damage could be an historical labor-demand result. It cannot serve as an excluded wage shock. [Original crop-calendar work](../calendar/README.md) · [DOL filing process](https://flag.dol.gov/programs/H-2A) |
| **5. Compare exposure to the 2018–19 retaliatory tariffs and farm payments.** | Retain as an output-demand question. The repository contains pre-2018 crop data, including the 2012 Cropland Data Layer. We found no ready country–product tariff and county–commodity payment exposure panel. The retaliating countries imposed the tariffs on US exports. Use partner-specific schedules and pre-policy export destinations. Convert duties and support to a common revenue unit, keeping price exposure and transfers separate. USDA's 2018 assistance was commodity-specific, while 2019 nonspecialty assistance used county rates per eligible acre. USDA attributes nearly 71% of estimated national agricultural export losses to soybeans. Historical H-2A exposure could differ, so measure overlap with H-2A crops and effective independent commodity shocks before estimating. Carbon's 25-shock target has not been met or tested. [USDA tariff estimates](https://ers.usda.gov/publications/102979) · [2018 assistance](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/news-events/news/08-29-2018/usda-announces-details-assistance-farmers-impacted-unjustified) · [2019 assistance](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/market_facilitation_program-fact_sheet-sept_2019.pdf) |
| **6. Exploit state agricultural overtime phase-ins.** | The existing Track 4 tested a 2015–18 baseline schedule exposure against 2011–25 quantities. It found only six treated states and 15 schedule changes, a failed lead test, and opposite signs for the ratio and count models. The California timetable differs at **26 total employees**. Workers requested on one application cannot assign a farm to the legal tier. The distinct idea is to study hours employers *advertise* at changing weekly caps. [Original schedule and quantity estimates](../../../markdowns/alternative_identification_exploration.md) · [California size rule](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Overtime-for-Agricultural-Workers-FAQ.html) |

**The two weather windows usually fall on different sides of filing.** The FY2020–25 median was 61 days from application receipt to requested start across 117,441 dated cases. We fixed each county's expected start using only 2009–11 filings, then linked 8,880 filings and 146,473 requested positions in 445 counties over 2021–24, retaining zero-filing county-years. Among cases with a freeze in the corresponding window, 2.9% had been received before the first early-window freeze and 87.6% before the first late-window freeze. Freezing temperatures are routine around many historical start dates. Requiring a county-average minimum at or below 0°C in a window that froze in at most 20% of 1982–2007 seasons left only **six early county-year events across four states and six late events across three**. The early estimate on log(1 + requested positions) was −0.001 (conventional state-cluster interval −0.500 to +0.498), the late estimate −0.074 (−0.167 to +0.020). With so few exposed states the intervals are descriptive. Extending the exposure check to **2012–25** on the same calendar yields just **22 early and 19 late unusual freezes**, each spread across seven states. The longer record helps less than the number of county-years suggests. A crop-specific, growth-stage-sensitive definition has a better agronomic interpretation. [New weather and filing pilot](../calendar/03_review_timing.R) · [NOAA daily data](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/nclimgrid-daily)

**The moving overtime cap has little support in posted schedules.** Among certified Washington crop, nursery and greenhouse laborer cases, zero of 313 advertised 55 or more hours in 2022. When the cap moved to 48 in 2023, eight of 331 advertised exactly 48 hours, versus six of 313 the year before. When it moved to 40 in 2024, the share above 40 fell from 9.1% to 2.9%, while roughly 34.5% advertised exactly 40 hours in both years. Oregon shares the AEWR region and began its own overtime phase-in in 2023, which limits its use as a control. The distinct Washington thresholds offer little evidence for a policy-induced bunching result. [New overtime pilot](../policy/06_review_overtime.R) · [Washington schedule](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/agriculture-policies/agricultural-overtime-faq) · [Oregon schedule](https://www.oregon.gov/boli/employers/Pages/minimum-wage-and-overtime-in-agriculture.aspx)

Carbon's leverage, dose and sample-size numbers describe different designs. For example, 13.3 is the effective state count in the 2009–10 wage-method exercise, 3.78 is an effective regional count for contract timing, and 142 is the number of baseline filers in the bond quantity comparison. The suggested F-statistic, power and overlap benchmarks are screening choices. A strong association between weather and insured losses or between enforcement and detainers would not establish a wage instrument. ## How to reproduce the new checks

Run from the repository root in the project R environment. The commands read cached sources and return aggregate results. The historical estimates in the parent report were produced before the FY2026 Q4 source refresh. The new diagnostics restrict application fiscal years, outcome years or filing windows as specified in each script. Filing identities can incorporate linkage evidence from a later vintage.

```sh
source scripts/pipeline_helpers.sh
configure_r_library
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/policy/03_review_bond.R /tmp/h2a-history-bond-review
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/packaging/02_review_companions.R /tmp/h2a-history-companions-review
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/occupations/03_review_text.R
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/policy/04_review_reset.R /tmp/h2a-history-reset-review
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/policy/05_review_aca.R /tmp/h2a-history-aca-review
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/policy/06_review_overtime.R /tmp/h2a-history-overtime-review
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/calendar/03_review_timing.R
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/reviews/01_source_support.R
```
