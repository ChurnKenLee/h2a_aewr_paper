Boilerplate copying into total need (78/91) mimics parity without real cuts.
- **Occupational requirements:** The 49 exits (28 equipment operators to truck drivers; +$7.75 raise) may reflect certifying-officer recoding rather than employer redesign, plus selective survival of trucking-heavy cells.
- **Wage resets:** Jumps to 95.8%–97.8% matching can reflect mandatory certification screening and pre-approval amendments. Lower downward matching (76.7% vs. 97.5%) likely reflects binding state minimums, prevailing wages, or ongoing contracts, not rigidity.
- **Usage:** Null timing estimates (−0.64%; +1.07% excluding Florida) can mask elasticities via certified-hours noise, start-date pull-forward, and Florida shocks dominating 3.78 effective regions.

### Six Ranked Tests and Designs (Public Data Only)
1. **Broadened bond-splitting audit (Highest; OFLC):** Expand companions to ±30 days and fuzzy-link address, agent, phone, and worksite. *Changes conclusion:* Companions absorbing ≥50% of −1 excess and smoothing combined crews prove paperwork splitting.
2. **Agent and missing-mass check (Very high; OFLC):** Test agent clustering of −1 spikes and whether missing mass above steps offsets −1 excess beyond round-number heaping. *Changes conclusion:* Balanced above-step holes across independent filers confirm threshold avoidance; agent-only spikes signal template artifacts.
3. **Receipt vs. certification wage test (High; OFLC, state wage schedules):** Split resets by receipt versus certification date and isolate AEWR drops unconstrained by state minimums or prevailing wages. *Changes conclusion:* Certification-only jumps imply administrative amendment; ~95% matching on unconstrained drops refutes rigidity.
4. **Reclassification text and lag audit (High; OFLC job orders):** Compare pre and post duty text (CDL/hauling), certification lags, and refilings for switchers versus stable cells. *Changes conclusion:* Unchanged duties with longer lags or refilings prove agency recoding; new duties with normal lags support employer restructuring.
5. **External staffing triangulation (Medium; QCEW NAICS 1151/111, NASS FLS):** Regress county-quarter agricultural-support employment and payroll on baseline bond exposure and leave-Florida-out AEWR shocks. *Changes conclusion:* Domestic hiring offsetting H-2A drops confirms substitution; flat payroll refutes real contraction.
6. **Border-county panel (Medium-low; OFLC, QCEW, Census adjacency):** Estimate pair-by-year fixed effects across contiguous counties straddling AEWR-region or state-minimum borders with leave-one-border-out inference. *Changes conclusion:* Achieving ≥15 effective borders, flat pre-trends, and MDE ≤2% establishes a tight zero or uncovers real demand sensitivity.
