# Recorded occupation and offered wages around the 2023 rule

A recurring filing entity could submit the same job title in the same state under a different recorded occupation. The clearest change around March 2023 ran toward occupations outside the six common field-and-livestock SOC codes. Of 49 cells moving out of the six-code group, 28 changed from agricultural equipment operator (45-2091) to heavy and tractor-trailer truck driver (53-3032). The 49 cells came from 48 filers in 20 states. Eighteen carried the exact normalized title *Agricultural Equipment Operator* in both seasons.

The February 2023 final rule introduced occupational wage schedules for other non-range jobs. The rule applied by job-order submission date. Disclosures supply case receipt dates, so the comparison uses the same months on each side with a gap around March 30. Combined duties, multi-state work and competing wage floors complicate legal assignment. [Federal Register rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/02/28/2023-03756/adverse-effect-wage-rate-methodology-for-the-temporary-employment-of-h-2a-nonimmigrants-in-non-range) · [Historical schedule, cohorts and court orders](../../identification_search/sources/aewr_2023_2025_occupation_notes.md)

The hypothesized switch toward the six codes shows little change at the rule date. Among 99 old other-occupation cells with a launch-date schedule gap above $5/hour, 11 moved into the six codes, versus 3 of 25 with a positive gap up to $5 and 4 of 11 with unresolved gaps. The event sample had no resolved nonpositive gaps. The small denominators preclude a sharp gradient test. A stricter R5 filer linkage produced 17 moves into and 47 moves out of the six. Matched May 1–August 15 seasons yielded 7/49 moving in during 2023 and 8/56 in the previous placebo (14.3% in each). In a fixed group of 5,482 cells observed across three seasons, moves into the six declined from 12 to 9 while moves out rose from 6 to 27. The overall frequency of switching out of the six was also around 0.50% in two earlier transitions, which weakens a claim of a unique break.

## What the forms already said about driving

The raw annual disclosures record a driver-requirement flag and a free-text additional-requirements field. Among the 28 equipment-operator → truck-driver cells, 26 marked driving as required on every case in both seasons. In 23 cells, every case with observed additional requirements in both seasons already mentioned a commercial license, commercial driving/motor vehicle, a tractor-trailer/semi-truck, public roads, or 26,001. Those terms are a fixed, exploratory text search. A mention can be qualified or negated. It establishes the language in the filing. It does not establish duties or hours behind the wheel.

| Occupation path | Cells with usable text both seasons | Mean share of cases mentioning trucking terms before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Equipment operator → truck driver | 28 | 82.1% | 89.3% |
| Equipment operator → equipment operator | 2,176 | 30.2% | 2.8% |
| Truck driver → truck driver | 46 | 81.2% | 77.5% |

The table gives each recurring cell equal weight and uses cases with nonblank text within each season. For recurring equipment-operator cells the same comparison moved from 24.9% to 24.8% in the preceding transition (2,045 paired cells) and from 2.5% to 2.8% in the following transition (2,963). In the rule transition, the comparable 45-2092 crop-farmworker group went from 9.9% to 9.6% (3,697). The stable equipment group retained nearly the same average share checking the separate driver-requirement box, 87.4% before and 86.7% after. The adjacent seasons had one and seven recorded equipment-operator → truck-driver transitions, respectively. Deleting each state in turn put the drop in equipment-operator wording between 24.6 and 29.0 percentage points. There were 518 different full texts among the 700 pre-rule equipment filings containing those words; the three most frequently repeated texts accounted for 48 filings. Similar templates can differ in exact wording. The equipment-operator contrast raises a separate question about jobs that kept their SOC but changed their written requirements. Linking the original job orders for switchers and stable controls would allow a blinded comparison of trucking duties, exceptions, attached addenda and standardized language. The disclosure field contains only additional requirements.

## Did prior trucking language predict subsequent requests?

The separate quantity pilot fixes an R7 filer and worksite-state cohort in January–February 2023, before the March rule. Each baseline cell requested at least one position and used only SOC 45-2091 on its filings in those months. All baseline titles and additional-requirements fields had to be observed. The exposed group comprises 583 filer-states with at least one trucking phrase, versus 1,277 without one. The median filer requested three positions in both groups. Exposure is then held fixed. Follow-up counts include title and SOC changes and use zero for a linked filer with no observed request in that state and window. Filers without later filings remain in the sample. A separately constructed January–February 2022 cohort of 422 exposed and 1,082 unexposed filers supplies a pre-rule placebo. The cohorts can overlap.

For each state with both groups, compute each group's total follow-up positions divided by its baseline positions. Weight the within-state difference by the exposed group's baseline positions in that state. Thus a one-percentage-point gap corresponds to one fewer or more follow-up requested position per 100 baseline positions. The unit is a program-use growth difference. The displayed approximate 95% intervals use the Student t reference and exact delete-one-state jackknife with reweighting. They rely on independence across states; cross-state filers and common agents may induce dependence. The January–February contrast captures changes in filing timing as well as scale.

The primary comparison included 582 exposed and 1,247 unexposed filer-states in 31 states. Per 100 baseline positions the exposed requested 59.7 in the following January–February, against 82.1 for the standardized comparison. Single-state deletions ranged from −28.2 to −8.1 points. The earlier placebo was −5.1 points (−28.7 to +18.6), and the January–February 2025 result for the same 2023 cohort was −14.6 points (−49.5 to +20.3). The full-year comparison uses filings from March 1 through the following February 28, keeping the original baseline cohorts and counting all titles and occupations in the baseline and follow-up years. This addresses filing-month shifts. The first follow-up includes the weeks before the March 2023 rule took effect. The full-year placebo was −5.4 points (−17.1 to +6.2); the year ending February 2025 was −18.6 (−50.4 to +13.2). Probability contrasts use exposed unit counts as weights. The return-probability placebo was +0.3 points (−5.7 to +6.3), and the January–February 2025 return gap was −0.1 (−7.2 to +6.9). The corresponding heavy-truck-filing gaps were −0.1 points (−0.3 to +0.1) and +2.0 (+0.3 to +3.8). These are several inspected outcomes and windows. Matching agents requires one identified baseline agent and retains both groups within that agent and state. The text sensitivity removes any unit using an exact normalized baseline requirements passage observed at three or more filers.

Among the entire frozen cohort, 6.0% of prior trucking-word units and 2.6% of other units filed a heavy-truck order in the following January–February. Among continuing filers with observed hourly offers, the median within-filer offer rose $0.99 in both groups. A minority mechanism among truck assignments fits the coding evidence. A common differential increase in labor costs across the exposed group remains unestablished. Words can proxy for crop, geography, the agent's drafting practices, size and seasonality. All contrasts are exploratory and reuse years and phrases already examined in the classification study.

## Reproduction and scope

From the project root:

```sh
source scripts/pipeline_helpers.sh
configure_r_library
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/occupations/01_switching.R
Rscript --vanilla explorations/historical_usage/occupations/02_predetermined_trucking.R
```

The direct-use pilot uses FY2021–25 case records for the annual sensitivity and FY2022–25 raw requirements for its pre-policy labels. It excludes nine conflicting case numbers across the annual windows. An unobserved later filer-state is zero recorded activity in this linkage and geography; identity changes, a move or missing worksite state can also produce it. Unknown certified totals in observed cases stay missing except for statuses explicitly denied or withdrawn. Requested counts are fully observed on the analysis records.

The switching script reads FY2018–FY2025 primary case disclosures and historical rows of the existing R5/R7 filer linkage. It also reads FY2021–25 raw driver and additional-requirements fields, the shared panel state crosswalk and the source-owned occupation and FLS schedules. It prints aggregate counts, destinations, wage comparisons, placebo results, requirement-field checks and exclusions. FY2021 lacks the exact additional-requirements field used in later workbooks, so observations from that vintage contribute no text measure to the earlier placebo. It compares all case statuses. It keeps primary case rows, removes exact duplicates and excludes six conflicting case numbers. States are the reported primary worksite states represented in the shared panel. Occupation codes retain their first six digits, grouped using the six statutory field/livestock SOCs. Titles match exactly after uppercase conversion, punctuation and whitespace normalization. The code does not infer that a changed title is the same job, or that an unchanged title guarantees fixed duties. Wage records must be hourly with a positive offer up to $100/hour. Each cell-season receives the median valid case offer. First-stage cost conversion and inference over an independent series of policy shocks are unavailable.

This is an exploratory classification and wage comparison developed after prior inspection of the 2023 reform. The earlier A3 quantity exercise lost 79% of dose variance after occupation controls, and its required shared-shock simulation offered no size-valid inference method. These descriptive transitions do not revise that assessment. [A3 findings](../../../markdowns/identification_search_report.md)
